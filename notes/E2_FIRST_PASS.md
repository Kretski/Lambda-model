# E2 — first pass: can horizon entanglement produce the Λk⁴ term?

*October 2026. Follows notes/HYPOTHESIS_ENTANGLEMENT_E1_E3.md. Markers: [known] = established
result in the literature or in a specific model; [estimate] = order-of-magnitude reasoning done
here; [hypothesis] = not established. No numerical work; Papers 1–3 unchanged.*

## 1. Entanglement does single out the model's preferred time [known]

The Minkowski vacuum restricted to a Rindler wedge is thermal with respect to boost time, and its
modular Hamiltonian generates boosts (Bisognano–Wichmann, J. Math. Phys. 16, 985 (1975); 17, 303
(1976); Unruh, Phys. Rev. D 14, 870 (1976)). For a black hole, the Hartle–Hawking state restricted
to the exterior is thermal with respect to Killing time (Hartle & Hawking, Phys. Rev. D 13, 2188
(1976)). The entanglement across the horizon therefore selects the static/Killing time, which is
the time the Lambda model uses (static observers, Paper 1; for Kerr the closely related ZAMO
slicing, Paper 2). This is the only argument found so far that **explains the origin of the
preferred frame** instead of imposing it.

Related framework for the separate "emergent time" idea [known]: the thermal time hypothesis
(Connes & Rovelli, Class. Quantum Grav. 11, 2899 (1994)) identifies physical time with the modular
flow of the state. That idea should start from this literature.

## 2. Vacuum entanglement alone does not give a k⁴ term [known principle]

An effective action obtained from a Lorentz-invariant state is Lorentz invariant; its
higher-derivative corrections are of the form $\Box^2$, which do not produce a preferred-frame
$k^4$ term in propagation. A preferred-frame $k^4$ term needs a state that breaks boost invariance,
i.e. the thermal state seen by the static observer of Section 1. A mechanism, if any, is
therefore "propagation through the thermal medium seen by static observers".

## 3. Size of the effect [estimate]

Corrections to propagation from gravitational interaction with that medium are suppressed by
$G$, i.e. of order $\ell_P^2$ times the square of the local temperature in inverse-length units.

- Far from the hole the temperature is the Hawking temperature (about $6\times10^{-8}$ K for one
  solar mass): negligible.
- Near the horizon the local (Tolman) temperature at proper distance $\rho$ is
  $T_{\rm loc} = \hbar c/(2\pi k_B \rho)$, independent of the mass. The suppression factor is then of order
  $(\ell_P/\rho)^2$, which reaches order unity only within about a Planck length of the horizon.

The induced coefficient is therefore either suppressed to the Planck scale or confined to a
Planck-thick layer: no observable $\Lambda$ (consistent with E1).

## 4. It inherits the horizon problem of A2(a) [conclusion from 1–3]

$T_{\rm loc}$ diverges where the lapse $N \to 0$, i.e. in the same static/Boyer–Lindquist frame that
fails at the horizon in A2(a). For freely falling observers the Hartle–Hawking state is regular,
so these divergences are very likely artefacts of the static frame rather than physical effects.

## Result

On the scale of the E1–E3 note: **outcome 2, close to outcome 3**. Entanglement plausibly explains
why the model uses the static/Killing time, but it does not give a $k^4$ term with an observable
coefficient, and the candidate mechanism is singular in the frame already found problematic at
the horizon.

What could change this [hypothesis]: a medium that is not thermal in the static frame and has its
own dynamics producing $\Lambda$ without $\ell_P^2$ suppression. None is known here; per the decision
rule of the E1–E3 note, no further work without such a concrete mechanism.
